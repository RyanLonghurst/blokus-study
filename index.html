About the study

This study is part of an undergraduate economics dissertation at the University of Edinburgh, run by Ryan Longhurst and supervised by [supervisor name]. It looks at how people solve puzzles in the board game Blokus, and when they choose to pay for hints. [Ethics approval reference.]

Points and the leaderboard

  • Every puzzle starts with 20 points. You lose points for every point your result falls short of the best possible result (a win by 1), and for any hints you buy. Some puzzles have higher stakes, where falling short costs double.

Need a reminder? How the puzzles work
  • The best result possible in every puzzle is a win by 1.
  • Each puzzle starts with 20 points. You lose 1 point for every point your result falls short of a win by 1: a draw costs 1, a loss by 2 costs 3. Hints you buy are also taken off.
  • Some puzzles have stakes x2: then each point short costs 2 instead of 1. Hints cost the same either way. You are told the stakes before each puzzle starts.
  • On your turn, the hint box shows what each hint costs for this move. Prices change from move to move, and on some moves there are no hints. Both are picked at random: a high price does not mean the move is hard, and "no hints" does not mean it is easy.
  • Which piece? marks the piece to use. Where? rings the square the piece should start from. Show the move shows exactly where the piece goes.
  • Hints are always right: they show a move that keeps the best result you can still get. They only help with the move you are about to make. You can buy none, one, or several.
  • You play each puzzle once. After each one you see where your score ranks against everyone who has played it, and at the end your overall ranking.
