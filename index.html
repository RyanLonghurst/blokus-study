About the study

This study is part of an undergraduate economics dissertation at the University of Edinburgh, run by Ryan Longhurst and supervised by [supervisor name]. It looks at how people learn to solve puzzles in the board game Blokus. [Ethics approval reference.]

What you will do

  • Play a short practice game to learn the controls.
  • Play Blokus puzzles against a computer that plays perfectly. There are nine puzzles, 10 minutes each. You can play each puzzle as many times as you like in its 10 minutes, and keep going after that if you want to.
  • The first puzzle takes about 15 minutes including the practice game. Every puzzle after that helps the study, so please do as many as you can, but you can stop whenever you like.
  • You do not need to do it all in one go. Your progress is saved in this browser, so you can stop after any puzzle, or even partway through one, and come back later to carry on where you left off. Use the same device and browser when you come back (not a private or incognito window).
  • The study works on a computer, tablet or phone.

What is recorded

  • Every move you make and when you make it, the pieces you pick up and turn, and when this page is in the background. Also your browser type and screen size.
  • Your name is not recorded. Your games are saved under a random code and stored on the Open Science Framework (OSF). [Who can access the data, how long it is kept, and whether anonymised data will be shared.]

Your rights

  • Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time by closing this page.
  • If you later want your data removed, email the researcher at with the participant code shown at the end of the study.
  • If you do not want to take part, simply close this page. Nothing is sent unless you agree below.

Prizes

  • For each of the nine puzzles there is a prize of [amount] for the participant with the best accuracy on that puzzle. Accuracy is judged on your best single attempt. Ties are decided by a random draw.
  • To enter, give your email address below. It is used only to contact winners, is stored separately from your game data, and is deleted once prizes have been paid. You can take part without entering.
