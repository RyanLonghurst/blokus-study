My name is Ryan Longhurst, and I am inviting you to take part in a research study for my economics dissertation. Before you decide, please read this information so you know why the research is being done and what it involves. Please contact me if anything is unclear.

This information sheet and the consent form both open from the first screen of the study.

What is the purpose of the study?

This study is part of my undergraduate dissertation in economics. Many decisions involve paying for information before acting, such as paying for advice or a report. The study looks at whether people buy information when it is worth more than its price. You play short puzzles in the board game Blokus against a computer that plays perfectly, and before each move you can spend points on hints. Because the game has been solved, the exact value of every hint is known.

Why have I been invited to take part?

The study is open to anyone aged 18 or over. You have received the link from me or from someone who knows me. You do not need to have played Blokus before.

Do I have to take part?

No. Taking part is entirely voluntary. You can stop at any time, without giving a reason, by closing the page. Nothing is sent until you have ticked the consent box and pressed Continue.

There is no payment, prize or reward for taking part. The points in the game have no monetary value; they only decide your place on the leaderboard.

What will happen if I take part?

  • You read this information and the consent form, and tick the consent box.
  • You watch a short tutorial video (77 seconds, with captions) and play one practice game.
  • You answer two quick questions on how the scoring works.
  • You choose a three-letter tag to appear on the leaderboard, or take a random name such as "Swift Otter". Please do not use your initials or any part of your name as your tag.
  • You play nine puzzles, one game each, against the computer. Each puzzle starts with 20 points. You lose points for every point your result falls short of the best possible result (a win by 1), and for any hints you buy. Hint prices are chosen at random before each move, and on some moves no hints are offered. Some puzzles count double.
  • After each puzzle you see your rank on that puzzle among everyone who has played it, with the two places above and below you. At the end you see your overall ranking.
  • You answer three short questions (how much Blokus you had played, whether you had played these puzzles before, and whether anyone helped you), with an optional comment box. Please do not put your name or other personal details in the comment.

Each puzzle takes a few minutes. There is no time limit, and you do not have to finish in one sitting: your progress is saved in your browser on your device, and you can come back later.

The puzzles download about 160 MB in total, so Wi-Fi is best.

What are the possible benefits of taking part?

There is no direct benefit to you, apart from the puzzles themselves. The results will help us understand how people decide when information is worth paying for.

What are the possible risks of taking part?

There are no significant risks. The computer plays perfectly, so you may lose some puzzles, which some people find frustrating. Your scores are compared with other players on the leaderboard, but only under your tag or random name. You can stop whenever you like.

What happens if I decide to withdraw?

You can stop at any time by closing the page. Data from puzzles you finished before stopping has already been sent and will be kept and used, unless you ask for it to be removed.

On the last screen of the study you are shown a six-character participant code. Please keep it. To have your data removed, email me with your code before 28 February 2027. Your data files and your rows on the leaderboard will then be deleted. After that date the data will have been anonymised and combined for analysis, so it can no longer be removed. Because I do not know who you are, I cannot find your data without your code.

Data protection and confidentiality

Who is responsible for your data. The University of Edinburgh, represented by me, is responsible for your data. Your data is used for research under the lawful basis of public task. The University's privacy notice for research participants explains this and your rights.

What is recorded. You are not asked for your name, email address or any contact details. Your data is saved under a random participant code. The study records:

  • every move you make and when, the hints you buy, the pieces you pick up and turn, and when the page is in the background or closed;
  • your answers to the scoring questions and the closing questions, and your comment if you write one;
  • your leaderboard tag or random name, and your scores and ranks;
  • your browser type, screen size, whether your device has a touch screen, your time zone offset, and whether you opened the page inside a social media app's browser. Browser type, screen size and time zone are used only to check whether the same device has taken part twice (everyone gets the same puzzles); touch support and in-app browser use are used only to check data quality.

This data is pseudonymous, not anonymous: it is linked by your participant code. It will not be combined with any other information about you; the only other data used is the computer's exact value for each move, which is not about you. The services below can see your device's IP address when your browser connects to them; the study itself does not record it.

Saved on your device. Your progress is saved in your browser's local storage on your own device, so you can come back later. It stays there until you clear your browser's site data. The study uses no cookies, analytics or advertising.

Some rights may be limited once the data has been anonymised. To use these rights, contact me, or the University's Data Protection Officer at dpo@ed.ac.uk.

International data transfers

Some of the services above are based outside the UK: GitHub and Google in the United States, and Zenodo at CERN in Switzerland. Your data may therefore be transferred outside the UK.

What will happen with the results of this study?

The results will be written up in my undergraduate dissertation and may be presented within the School of Economics. Only overall results will be reported. No individual will be identifiable, and tags, random names, participant codes and comments will not be published.

Need a reminder? How the puzzles work
  • The best result possible in every puzzle is a win by 1.
  • Each puzzle starts with 20 points. You lose 1 point for every point your result falls short of a win by 1: a draw costs 1, a loss by 2 costs 3. Hints you buy are also taken off.
  • Some puzzles have stakes x2: then each point short costs 2 instead of 1. Hints cost the same either way. You are told the stakes before each puzzle starts.
  • On your turn, the hint box shows what each hint costs for this move. Prices change from move to move, and on some moves there are no hints. Both are picked at random: a high price does not mean the move is hard, and "no hints" does not mean it is easy.
  • Which piece? marks the piece to use. Where? rings the square the piece should start from. Show the move shows exactly where the piece goes.
  • Hints are always right: they show a move that keeps the best result you can still get. They only help with the move you are about to make. You can buy none, one, or several.
  • You play each puzzle once. After each one you see where your score ranks against everyone who has played it, and at the end your overall ranking.
